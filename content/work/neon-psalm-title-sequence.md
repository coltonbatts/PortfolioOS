## Project Overview

*Neon Psalm* is an experimental short film about faith, technology, and the spaces between. The director needed a title sequence that set the tone—something that felt sacred and synthetic, reverent and retrofuture.

## Concept

The sequence explores the collision of religious iconography and neon aesthetics. Byzantine mosaics flicker like dying screens. Latin liturgy glitches into binary. Stained glass windows rendered in scan lines.

The idea: what does divinity look like in a world built on circuits?

## Visual Language

### Typography

I used a custom blackletter typeface that I distressed and digitized—medieval letterforms corrupted by pixel decay. Each title card degrades slightly, as if the signal is failing. The text doesn't just appear; it struggles to exist.

### Color & Light

Deep blacks with electric blues, magentas, and toxic greens. The palette is deliberately uncomfortable—neon signs in a cathedral, holy light through a CRT screen.

### Motion & Pacing

Slow, deliberate, almost liturgical. The camera moves like a procession through digital ruins. Nothing rushes. Every frame holds weight.

## Technical Approach

- **After Effects**: Primary animation and compositing
- **Photoshop**: Texture creation and glitch assets
- **Cinema 4D**: 3D environments and architectural elements
- **Premiere Pro**: Final sequence edit and color grading

## Sound Design Collaboration

Worked closely with the sound designer to sync motion with audio—each title card timed to electronic drones and distant choir samples. The visuals and sound are inseparable.

## Results

The sequence premiered at several indie film festivals and became the film's calling card. It's been featured in motion design galleries and inspired a series of art prints.

## Reflection

This project reminded me why I love title sequences. You get 90 seconds to create a mood, establish a world, and make people *feel* something before the story even starts. It's pure distilled atmosphere.
